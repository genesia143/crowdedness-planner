# Model-improvement experiments: development period only

The team's rule is that October 25–31 must never be used to build or tune a model. That week was previously evaluated for an earlier candidate and is not a fresh independent test for a revised candidate. The experiments below used October 1–21 training and/or October 22–24 development records; no new model was trained on October 25–31.

- **Magnitude and day-to-day stability:** an illustrative ±25% rule labels fewer supported hours as busier, but compares against a differently defined target than rank and has not been shown to improve accuracy. The positive-only same-training-days stability check has selection and denominator biases. See [holiday and magnitude study](holiday-magnitude-study.md).
- **Holiday exclusion:** on the same supported weekday cells, excluding October 2 and 20 in both observed passengers and scheduled crossings changed magnitude-target agreement from 65.14% to 65.39%. Support was fixed from the original model; correlated ticket-sample results do not establish a meaningful improvement in crowding prediction.
- **Shrinkage and neighbouring segments:** fixed trial rules had slightly higher sample-rate MAE than the baseline on the same development cells; see [smoothing experiment](smoothing-development-experiment.md). This does not exclude other choices, but provides no basis to deploy these two settings.
- **Stop geography:** on a convenience set of human-reviewed route/name/role mappings with global exact GTFS names and at least one route stop within 300 m, the nearest candidate agreed for 31 of 44 keys and disagreed for 13; 14 had multiple candidates. 184 of 241 reviewed keys had no exact global GTFS name. These are reviewed stop-role keys already incorporated into the baseline, not newly recovered tickets, and no ordered path was validated. Treat proximity as a review aid, not an automatic match. A fare check still requires authoritative fare-stage, fare-version and group-ticket rules.
- **Exit sensitivity:** among 916,872 matched training-period tickets, moving eligible destinations one stop earlier reduced aggregate sample passenger-segments by 979,169 (7.80%); moving later increased them by 816,328 (6.51%). A one-segment trip cannot be shortened, and 159,338 tickets had no unique next stop or terminated. These are aggregate exposure changes, not label changes, and must not be described as robustness of prediction labels. Script: `scripts/exit_stop_sensitivity.py`.
- **Rotating weeks:** weekday-only decisive-pair concordance on held-out development weeks was 82.6%, 84.0%, and 82.5%, with the sample and correlation caveats in [rotating-week results](rotating-week-development.md). Weekend strict support cannot be met using only two training weekends in each fold.
- **Independent field observations:** not yet collected. A proposed [field-check protocol](field-study/protocol.md) defines measurement and safeguards; 30–50 convenience observations alone cannot validate citywide performance.

No proposed threshold, smoothing, holiday, geographic match or exit-shift rule was added to the production model on the basis of these experiments. They do not demonstrate actual occupancy accuracy. Remaining work includes label-level exit sensitivity, validated fare rules, day-level and out-of-sample stability, production-worthy coverage and independent passenger observations. A new independent test period is needed to measure a revised model after development decisions.
