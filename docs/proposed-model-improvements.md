# Review of proposed model improvements

The October 25–31 ticket target has already been used to evaluate the previous candidate. It is no longer a sealed, independent final test for model changes. All new model comparisons here use development data only; they estimate ticket-sample patterns, not bus occupancy.

1. **Magnitude rather than forced ranks:** Valid concern. Added `scripts/compare_magnitude_labels.py` as an experiment, not as a production rule. It compares an illustrative ±25% threshold around the *unweighted mean of supported hours* with rank labels on the same supported development-period cells. For weekdays, 45,420/169,389 candidate hours were busier versus 55,714/169,389 under ranking; Saturday, 6,920/27,522 versus 8,955/27,522. The candidate's three-band agreement with its own magnitude-defined ticket target was 110,371/169,389 weekdays and 16,136/27,522 Saturdays. Rank agreement is against a different, rank-defined target: those percentages cannot establish which definition is more accurate. The supported-hour mean is not a full-day or service-weighted average, and this experiment does not establish the required across-day stability. There is no Sunday target in the tuning window. The 25% threshold is a hypothesis, not a validated standard.
2. **Neighbouring segments:** Plausible but risky near stops with many boardings/alightings, route branches, gaps or opposing directions. Study consecutive segments only on compatible route patterns, preserve boundaries and compare performance against a no-smoothing baseline; do not assume zero-data segments are quiet or automatically trustworthy.
3. **Shrinkage:** Plausible. Compare evidence-weighted estimates and alternatives with matching coverage and reliability checks. Shrinking toward a flat all-day mean can suppress real peaks, and shrinkage alone does not provide a calibrated confidence probability. Retain an insufficient-evidence option.
4. **Holidays:** Delhi's 2026 government holiday list names 2 October Gandhi Jayanti, 20 October Dussehra, and 26 October Maharishi Valmiki's birthday. Test exclusion from ordinary-weekday training with date-aligned timetable denominators; do not automatically reclassify holidays as Sundays. 26 October lies in the already-used final week. A government holiday calendar does not prove actual bus service or ridership levels.
5. **Spatial stop matching:** Nearby names and route-constrained spatial candidates are useful for a reviewed candidate pipeline. 300 m and nearest stop alone do not prove equivalence; opposite directions, parallel roads and inaccessible crossings can cause false matches. Fare rules, stage definitions, transfers, group size and price validity must be verified before using fare as a check. Validate on manually reviewed examples before using new matches for training.
6. **Field checks:** High value. Counts of passengers or occupancy categories, bus identity, capacity definition, route, direction, time, location and observer protocol would provide independent evidence. A 30–50 bus convenience sample is useful exploratory evidence but cannot by itself guarantee network-wide accuracy; include peak/off-peak and varied locations and protect individual privacy.
7. **Exit sensitivity and rotating evaluation:** Both valid. Rebuild records from ticket-level journeys and shift exits only to legal ordered stops on the same pattern; report tickets affected and invalid cases. Compare labels/coverage rather than assume negligible change. Rotating weeks gives multiple checks but folds share training data and are correlated, not independent trials. Maintain strict date boundaries, route matching and no use of the already-examined final week as fresh holdout.
